Every word is built from parts. This guide splits 13 words into a prefix, a root word and a suffix. Then you can learn them and test yourself.

The three parts of a word

PrefixComes at the start. It changes the meaning of the word.
Root wordThe main part. It carries the core meaning.
SuffixComes at the end. It changes the job of the word, or the tense.
un + kind + ness = unkindness

Un means not. Kind means gentle and caring. Ness means the state of being. So unkindness means the state of not being kind.

Some roots are called bound roots. That means they cannot stand alone as a word, like tract in distracted. One word here, stereophonic, starts with a combining form instead of a prefix. A combining form is a word part from Greek that works a bit like a prefix.

Tap a word to split it into its parts and see what it means.

Same letters, different jobs

The prefix in can mean into or not. In intimidated, in means into. In inevitable, in means not. Read the rest of the word and the sentence to decide which one it is.

Spelling pattern: in changes to im before the letters m, b and p. That is why you see im in immense and impaled.

Spelling changes when a suffix is added

Some roots end in e. When the suffix ed is added, the two e letters join into one. Increase plus ed is increased, not increaseed. In intimidated, ate plus ed is ated.

Notes for teachers and parents

  • The origins of distracted, intimidated, inevitable, immense, increased, stereophonic and impaled were checked against Etymonline. The other six were not checked, and each card says which it is. Check them in a dictionary before teaching them as fact.
  • Dictionaries can split some words differently. Immense, inevitable, increased and stereophonic are the most debatable. The splits here follow the origin given on each card.
